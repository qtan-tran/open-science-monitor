1. Open Science and the Effects of Generative Artificial Intelligence in Scientific Exchange

new · European Parliament STOA / EPRS · Europe · study

oa-policyresearch-evidencemultilingualism

Published: 2026-10-19 · Recorded: 2026-10-23

Sources: Full study (EPRS PDF) (primary) · Official ERA summary (secondary)

What changed: The report evaluates how generative AI could either strengthen accessibility, multilingual exchange, and discoverability or undermine Open Science through obscured provenance, infrastructure dependency, unequal capabilities, and "synthetic overload": more generated content than researchers and systems can reliably verify.

Why it matters: Its policy options treat scientific exchange as public-interest infrastructure. Openness is reformulated as a combination of accessibility, provenance, reproducibility, inclusion, skills, and institutional capacity, not merely unrestricted availability. It provides a timely European policy vocabulary connecting Open Science, AI-mediated publishing, research integrity, and infrastructure governance.

3. The EOSC Federation Enters Its Operational Phase

new · European Commission DG Research and Innovation · Europe · announcement · policy pol-eosc

infrastructureresearch-data

Published: 2026-10-16 · Recorded: 2026-10-23

Sources: Commission implementation update (primary)

What changed: The first 13 EOSC Nodes have begun operating, extending the Federation beyond the central EU Node. Twenty-seven national, thematic, and e-infrastructure Nodes are joining through two waves, using shared federating capabilities and interoperable services.

Why it matters: This is a concrete transition from Open Science infrastructure policy to distributed service delivery. The unresolved policy questions now concern interoperability, user participation, security, data protection, sustainability, and how nationally controlled resources are coordinated without eliminating local autonomy.

What changed: Responding to the design of European Partnerships under FP10, EUA calls for EOSC to remain a distinct partnership with its own work programme. It characterises EOSC as a collectively funded, community-owned research commons connecting distributed resources while preserving national and institutional ownership.

Why it matters: Following the LIBER position, this shows convergence between libraries and universities around a specific post-2027 governance model. It also places Open Science infrastructure inside the broader contest over whether FP10 funding should prioritise open collaborative research or industrial deployment.
