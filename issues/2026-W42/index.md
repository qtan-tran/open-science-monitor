## 1. Open Access Scholarly Publishing and Article Processing Charges: Perspectives and Experiences of Researchers from Africa

**Academy of Science of South Africa (ASSAf)** — new · Africa · topics: apc-bpc, diamond-oa, equity, research-evidence · report

Published: 2026-09-02 · Recorded: 2026-10-16

Sources: [Full report and repository record (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.17159/assaf.2026/127) (primary)

**What changed:** Based on responses from 761 researchers across Africa, the report examines how APCs, journal prestige, institutional support, and knowledge of OA routes affect publication choices. It finds substantial interest in Diamond OA but persistent problems with visibility, infrastructure, sustainability, and recognition in evaluation systems. The report was first published on 2 September and formally highlighted by ASSAf on 15 October 2026.

**Why it matters:** Its central conclusion is analytically useful: the problem is not open access itself but how open access is financed and embedded in research assessment. It provides comparative evidence connecting author-level journal selection to APC inequalities and prestige incentives, directly relevant to interviews and policy design on author behaviour in Africa.

## 2. LIBER Position on the Future of the European Open Science Cloud

**LIBER (Association of European Research Libraries)** — new · Europe · topics: infrastructure, oa-policy, funder-mandates · position

Published: 2026-10-13 · Recorded: 2026-10-16

Sources: [Position paper (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23336117) (primary)

**What changed:** LIBER proposes maintaining EOSC beyond 2027 as a distinct, Work Programme-based European Partnership under FP10. It asks that research libraries be recognised as EOSC supporting infrastructure, included in governance and implementation, and made directly eligible in future funding calls.

**Why it matters:** The paper identifies an institutional "last-mile" problem: European infrastructure may exist technically without organisations capable of integrating it into everyday research practice. Its proposed solution assigns libraries a formal intermediary role rather than treating them merely as service users.

## 3. Aligning Research Assessment, Scholarly Communication, and Open Infrastructure: Funders' Perspectives

**DORA Funder Community of Practice** — new · Global · topics: research-assessment, indigenous-data, research-evidence · analysis

Published: 2026-10-14 · Recorded: 2026-10-16

Sources: [DORA analysis](https://sfdora.org/2026/10/14/funders-rra-scholarly-communication-open-infrastructure/) (primary)

**What changed:** Discussions involving funders and the Barcelona Declaration initiative connect responsible assessment to open funding metadata. Proposed actions include persistent grant identifiers, open grant metadata through APIs, standardised funding acknowledgements, and automated validation across funder and publisher systems. A readiness survey covers 39 funders in 27 countries.

**Why it matters:** The analysis usefully distinguishes technical openness from responsible assessment: collecting more interoperable metadata does not itself prevent reductive metrics. It also identifies practical barriers, including staff capacity, standardisation, legacy systems, and tensions between individual-attribution metadata and Indigenous collective governance.

Policy thread: `pol-rra`

Event ID: `evt-dora-funders-rra`

## 4. Diamond OA Policy Forum Europe: Strategies for Funding Diamond OA in Europe

**ALMASI project; contributors from Slovenia, ANR, TU Delft, Université de Lorraine, TIB and SCOSS** — new · Europe · topics: diamond-oa, business-models, funder-mandates · materials

Published: 2026-10-12 · Recorded: 2026-10-16

Sources: [Complete slide collection (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23301451) (primary)

**What changed:** The collection places six funding approaches side by side: Slovenian national policy, the French ANR perspective, a university-library model, the AEGIS-OA protection project, TIB's German KOALA consortial-financing service, and SCOSS's international coordination model.

**Why it matters:** These materials expose the institutional choices hidden inside the generic injunction to "fund Diamond OA": direct public subsidy, library portfolio funding, consortial pledging, risk pooling, or internationally coordinated support. The KOALA case is especially useful for Germany because it translates dispersed library budgets into multi-year collective financing.
