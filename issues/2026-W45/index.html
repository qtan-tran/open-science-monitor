1. Open Research Europe opens to researchers across eleven countries

new · European Commission, DFG and participating organisations · Europe · announcement · policy pol-ore

infrastructurediamond-oabusiness-models

Published: 2026-11-02 · In force from: 2026-11-02 · Recorded: 2026-11-06

Sources: DFG announcement (primary) · European Commission announcement (primary)

What changed: Open Research Europe has moved from preparation to operation. Researchers affiliated with institutions in eleven participating countries, including Germany, can now submit without either EU-project funding or author fees. The platform accepts articles, registered reports, data notes, case studies, and essays, using a Publish-Review-Curate model with public reviews. CERN operates the service.

Why it matters: This is a substantial implementation experiment in collectively financed, fee-free European publishing. Sixteen funders and research organisations plus the Commission have committed nearly EUR 17 million for 2026-2031, including up to EUR 10 million from the Commission. For Germany, country-level participation effectively creates a national publishing entitlement independent of individual DFG funding.

2. Should NIH Restrict the Use of Grant Funds for Publication Costs? An Analysis of Public Comments

new · Robert M. Kaplan, Health Affairs Scholar · North America · study · policy pol-nih-pub-costs

apc-bpcconsultationsequityresearch-evidence

Published: 2026-11-05 · Recorded: 2026-11-06

Sources: Article (DOI) (primary) · NIH compiled public comments and data (primary)

What changed: The study codes all 914 submissions to NIH's consultation on restricting allowable publication costs. Almost half criticised APCs; recurring concerns included publisher concentration, unpaid editorial labour, and inequitable effects on smaller laboratories and early-career researchers. Organisations tended to frame APCs structurally, while individual researchers emphasised immediate financial burdens.

Why it matters: The results challenge the assumption that an APC ceiling alone constitutes effective OA cost policy. Respondents feared that blunt grant-level caps could shift costs onto researchers, disadvantage smaller nonprofit journals, or strengthen publishers with greater economies of scale. The paper instead points toward coordinated procurement, institutional publishing infrastructure, Diamond OA, and formal recognition of reviewing labour. Methodological caution: respondents were self-selected and most comments were initially classified using AI, with human validation limited to a 5% sample.

3. Making Responsible Research Assessment Work through Scholarly Communication and Open Infrastructures

new · OpenAIRE · Global · analysis · policy pol-rra

research-assessmentinfrastructure

Published: 2026-11-02 · Recorded: 2026-11-06

Sources: OpenAIRE analysis (primary)

What changed: Drawing on an OpenAIRE-DORA consultation, the analysis identifies implementation needs for DORA's forthcoming guide to responsible assessment in scholarly communication. Participants emphasised changes to doctoral and selection committees, recognition of data, software and metadata, shared training for academic leaders and support staff, and alignment between editorial policy, infrastructure, and evaluation criteria.

Why it matters: Its strongest point is that infrastructure cannot reform assessment by itself. Greater visibility of diverse outputs matters only when committees change their criteria and routines; formal institutional policies can otherwise coexist with continuing reliance on journal brands and citation counts.
